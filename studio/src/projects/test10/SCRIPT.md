# Test10 — Eiffel Tower, sold twice

## Hook gate
Drafts across required patterns: question “Who sold Paris’s tower?”; false assumption “The city owned the sale”; impossible claim “The Eiffel Tower was sold for scrap. Twice.”; number shock “One tower, two buyers, one fake sale”; urgency “Paris nearly lost its tower”; direct address “Would you buy the Eiffel Tower?”; contradiction “A monument was sold as scrap”; weird action “A man auctioned a landmark.” Winner cold-read: **“The Eiffel Tower was sold for scrap. Twice.”** Understandable without context, one pattern, true, under 14 words, open question: who could do that?

## Facts + source
In 1925, Victor Lustig posed as a French official and convinced scrap-metal dealers the Eiffel Tower was to be dismantled and sold. André Poisson paid; embarrassed, he did not report the fraud. Lustig returned and tried again; the second mark alerted police. Sources: Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Victor Lustig” (https://www.britannica.com/biography/Victor-Lustig); Smithsonian Magazine, “The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower” (https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/man-who-sold-eiffel-tower-180956416/).
